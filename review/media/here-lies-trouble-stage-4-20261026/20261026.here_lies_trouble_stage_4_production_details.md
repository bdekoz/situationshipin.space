## 6. Conversational motion specification

Gaze is staged from speaker turns, with soft transitions and pauses:

1. Eyes lead a modest head turn toward the addressee; the torso and feet
   continue the walk. The unicorn may look slightly down toward the dog,
   and the dog slightly up toward the unicorn.
2. The listener returns the look after about 0.25 seconds. Blend the turn
   over roughly 0.6 seconds, hold briefly, then ease back to the path.
3. Add an occasional small listener nod, ear response, or head tilt. A
   spoken syllable is not a trigger for a body bounce or a head movement.
4. At a turn handoff, attention shifts naturally to the next speaker.
   Long speeches include intervals of looking ahead; avoid sustained
   staring, repeated synchronized nods, or an attentive pose on every word.

Working limits: at least 2.5 seconds between gaze onsets, around 1.2
seconds of gaze hold, and at most three primary interaction cues in a
15-second clip. These are staging targets, not measurements guaranteed
by the model. The initial addressee inference is the previous different
speaker, or the next different speaker for the opening. Review that
inference where the text clearly addresses the group or another person.

There is no established exact lip synchronization in the current method.
Speech is synthesized separately. Evaluate gaze and listening first;
do not introduce exaggerated mouth animation as an accidental substitute.
This describes the frozen Stage 4 method. Stage 5 explicitly adds speaking
mouth animation under section 12; do not carry this omission into its new
prompt or change historical Stage 4 requests.
