Root causes established from code and source inspection

  1. Every v6 listener inherited the same speech-relative 7.6-second glance clock, offset by only 0.18 seconds per animal. Every turn used the same profile-to-front atlas; target labels did not supply the missing away view.
  2. The unicorn's 22-frame source selection did not contain a matching full stride. Its last pose was interpolated back to a substantially different first pose. A source-cycle search finds a better 36-frame selection.
  3. Optical flow used RGB outside the source alpha mask, allowing the moving scenery to influence interpolation. Disagreeing limbs became translucent double edges. The detached neutral head also introduced a moving neck join.
  4. Some unicorn limb pixels were genuinely hidden by the source dog/cat. Segmentation and opaque masks cannot recover observed anatomy there; existing explicit occlusion completions remain reviewable approximations.

Selected implementation

  • Use complete walking surfaces with their attached neutral heads, applying speech motion to the lower jaw in that same image. Preserve dog/cat cycle lengths: 35 and 18 output frames. Use unicorn source frames 5–40 as a complete 66-output-frame cycle, matching the shared travel speed.
  • Compute flow on masked animal RGB over a neutral field. Use an opaque interior silhouette and repair narrow matte cracks/edge contamination.
  • Evaluate conversation eligibility using authored ground-contact roots, longitudinal position and neighboring trail lanes, not eye height.
  • Select at most one speaker/listener pair, with individual glance timing. Require reciprocal toward/away view coverage. With the existing staggered staging, forward attention is the expected result. Unsupported turns stay forward rather than substituting a camera-facing pose.

The saved source library supplies profile-to-camera turns, not reciprocal away-from-camera views. This revision can stop incorrect group camera turns; it does not invent missing head surfaces or silently claim mutual eye contact.

The source unicorn's stance displacement is approximately 8 pixels per source frame. Retiming 36 source frames into 66 output frames gives approximately 8 × 36 / 66 × 24 = 104.73 pixels/s against 105-pixel/s scenery travel. This is a source-image calibration, not a recovered 3D gait measurement.

Each output quadruped is one complete RGBA walking pose, including its neutral head, neck, torso and visible limbs. There is no detached neutral head pasted over a moving neck and no independently composited leg rectangle. All 119 selected cycle phases have one connected foreground component at alpha >128, with fully opaque interiors more than two pixels inside the silhouette.

Preparation computes bidirectional Farneback flow on masked animal RGB over a uniform gray field. It warps premultiplied color/alpha, selects a single material silhouette, closes only narrow three-pixel cracks, fills enclosed holes no larger than 24 pixels, and removes detached fragments below 256 pixels. Large spaces between legs stay open. A 0.45-pixel Gaussian edge softens the outline without making limb interiors translucent. Bright contamination on dark lower limbs is replaced only where an observed dark animal pixel lies within eight pixels; per-phase pixel counts are saved in atlas-audit.json.

The unicorn still contains explicit short completions where the source dog or cat hid a limb. Those pixels are approximations, not newly observed anatomy. Removing small detached fragments avoids floating hoof flecks; it does not prove that every hidden hoof has been anatomically reconstructed.

The walking head's lower lip is registered separately from the old detached head atlas. The dog uses a six-point curve along its actual smile. Unicorn and cat use their lower-muzzle endpoints. Masked flow transports these landmarks through each pose cycle. Mouth changes are confined to a small jaw rectangle, which replaces the corresponding pixels directly. Remapping or compositing the whole body during speech would perturb unrelated antialiased edges.

The four existing speech envelopes are preserved exactly. Mouth apertures remain energy-driven, with the prior silence gating and discrete opening levels; this is not phoneme synthesis. Only the active speaker's mouth opens.

Conversation behavior and its current limit

Travel runs toward image-right. Authored roots near each animal's torso/ground contact define longitudinal progress and lane depth. Eye height is excluded: a tall unicorn and a small dog can occupy neighboring lanes without their eyes sharing a screen row.

Two grounded actors can be a conversation pair only when their longitudinal separation is less than 60 pixels and their lane separation is 12–130 pixels. The turn weight fades between 40 and 60 longitudinal pixels. Geometry is reevaluated every frame. An existing eligible partner is retained to prevent rapid switching; otherwise the nearest eligible partner is chosen. Everyone outside that single pair remains forward. Speaker/listener glance events use different onset/return times and deterministic per-turn seeds.

The thresholds and root registrations are compiled from hlt_local_stage5_v7_gaze.py and the preparation script. gaze-policy.json records those decisions; it is not a live configuration file. For a new candidate, change the compiler deliberately and regenerate performance.json before freezing, rather than editing only the policy receipt.

An actual pair requires a toward-camera view for its farther member and an away-from-camera view for its nearer member. The source library has only the former. Missing reciprocal coverage keeps both neutral. The current renderer also rejects an unexpected active turn rather than silently substituting a frontal atlas. Adding views requires a new candidate and a registered reciprocal head renderer; changing a coverage flag alone is insufficient.

The unchanged ten-minute formation contains zero eligible abreast pair frames. All 14,400 controls therefore retain forward gaze, including silence. There are no synchronized camera turns or scripted wildlife-directed turns. The incidental animals themselves retain their accepted event animation. Synthetic policy fixtures demonstrate pair eligibility, ahead/behind exclusion, missing-view rejection and independently timed reciprocal events; those fixtures are not footage proving mutual eye contact.
